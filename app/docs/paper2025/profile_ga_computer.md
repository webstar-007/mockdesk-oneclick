### GA summary
- Subject counts: History 4 (Q2, 8, 15, 23); Polity/Law 4 (Q3, 7, 16, 19); Economy 4 (Q1, 6, 18, 25); Sports 3 (Q5, 12, 17); Geography 2 (Q14, 20); Biology/Environment 2 (Q9, 10); Science-Tech/Space 2 (Q11, 24); Awards/Culture/Books 2 (Q4, 21); Schemes 1 (Q22); International orgs 1 (Q13). No pure chemistry/physics, no static-GK capitals/dances/dams style items.
- Current affairs: 8 clear (Q3, 5, 7, 11, 12, 21, 22, 24) plus 2 topical-static (Q4, Q10), so about 32-40%. Time window: Jul 2024 to Dec 2025 (about 18 months before the exam), with the newest item (FIH Junior World Cup) only about 6 weeks old at exam date. Themes: new criminal law BNS, 2025 VP change, space missions (NISAR, SPHEREx, MACE), Paris 2024, hockey event hosted in India, Pulitzer, a 2025 education/culture mission.
- Depth: mostly concept-level facts with named detail only where the news item is prominent. Higher depth: exact dates and host cities (Q5), exact section number and years (Q3), instrument bands (Q24), telescope location (Q11). Static items test standard NCERT/Lucent facts plus one twist (Q2 "incorrect" form, Q14 swapped location).
- Styles: statement-based "correct/incorrect" 7 (Q2, 7, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16, plus Q15 as pick-the-correct-statement), match-the-following 1 (Q19), pair-matching "which pair is correct" 1 (Q20), direct single-fact MCQ about 13, conceptual "primary objective/focus" about 5 (Q1, 8, 18, 22, 23). Statement items use the standard 4 options (only 1 / only 2 / both / neither). Distractors are plausible, not absurd.

### 10 rules for matching GA difficulty
1. Mix: about 8-10 current-affairs, 15-17 static; about 4 history, 4 polity/law, 4 economy, 3 sports, 2 each geography, bio/env, sci-tech, awards.
2. Keep the average difficulty about 2.6; most items should be 2-3, only 2-3 items at level 4, none that need obscure trivia.
3. Make about 7-8 questions statement-based (2 statements, 4 standard options) and exactly 1 match-the-following.
4. Current items must come from the 18 months before the exam, with 2-3 from the last 2 months.
5. For sports, ask host, venue, dates or a record-style claim about a major event; add one rules/format static item (quarters, players, pitch size).
6. For polity, use articles, schedules, and constitutional offices; for law, use section-level questions on new laws (BNS/BNSS/BSA).
7. For economy, use standard concepts (deficits, plan models, HDI components, 1991 reforms), not data figures.
8. For history, test purpose/feature of a policy or ruler's measure (Delhi Sultanate, East India Company) and one INCORRECT-statement item.
9. Include one "twist" in a statement (swap a state, year or body) rather than inventing fictional facts.
10. Distractors must be plausible neighbours of the right answer (same domain, same era), and every option set is single-answer with no overlap.

### 5 ways to be slightly harder
1. Raise two statement items to 3 statements with combination options (1 and 3 only, etc.).
2. Ask for exact numbers where this paper asks for concepts (section number, article, year, dates, edition number).
3. Add chronological-order or "arrange in order" items for history/missions.
4. Make current items less headline-level: venue and runner-up, specific instrument or component, scheme ministry and year.
5. Use a "which is NOT correct" form on a static match or pair list (2-3 near-identical distractors).

### Computer summary
- Topic counts: MS Office/Windows 7 (Excel 3: Q1, 13, 14; Word 2: Q2, 3; PowerPoint 1: Q11; Windows shortcut 1: Q10); Security/cryptography/malware 4 (Q7, 15, 18, 19); Computer organisation (control unit, address lines, IR) 3 (Q4, 8, 9); Networking/data communication 2 (Q5, 6); advanced/IT-infra items 3 (OLE/COM Q12, copy-on-write storage Q16, HDMI vs DisplayPort Q17); cloud/file basics 1 (Q20). Absent: DBMS/SQL, number-system conversions (only a memory-addressing calculation), OS concepts (scheduling, memory management), internet browsing basics.
- Level: a mix of basic (OSI layer, COUNTIF, Filter, compression, Trojan) and "BTech-flavoured" items (microprogramming, RTS/CTS handshaking, COM/OLE, CoW snapshots). Office questions are scenario-based ("a user wants to...") more than raw definition.
- Style: single-fact MCQs, 4 options, no statement or match items, plenty of "what is the primary purpose/advantage" wording; two scenario items (Q2, Q14), one numeric (Q8). Distractors are mostly in the same domain.

### 10 rules for matching Computer difficulty
1. Use about 7 MS Office/Windows items (3 Excel, 2 Word, 1 PPT, 1 Windows shortcut), at least 2 of them scenario-based.
2. Use about 4 security/crypto/malware items (email security, encryption benefit, symmetric vs asymmetric, malware type).
3. Use 3 computer-organisation items (control unit, registers, address-line calculation).
4. Use 2 networking items (OSI layer, serial communication), and 3 "IT-infra/advanced" items.
5. Keep 4 trivially easy items (level 1), about 6 at level 2, about 7 at level 3, about 3 at level 4.
6. Ask "primary purpose/advantage/limitation" with 4 plausible options in the same domain.
7. For shortcuts, choose less common but standard ones (Alt+Enter, Win+T), not Ctrl+C.
8. For Excel, test the function that fits the task (COUNTIF vs COUNT/COUNTBLANK/SUMIF) or tool choice (Filter vs Sort).
9. Numeric items should be one-step (2^n addressing), not multi-step.
10. No code-output, SQL or binary-conversion heavy items, since the paper did not use them; keep every question answerable in under 30 seconds.

### 5 ways to be slightly harder
1. Add a two-step calculation (memory of 8K x 16 with address and data lines, or binary/hex conversion plus addressing).
2. Use closer distractors (IMAP vs POP3 vs S/MIME vs TLS; COUNTIFS vs COUNTIF vs SUMPRODUCT).
3. Add a DBMS item (keys, normalisation, ACID) and an OS item (paging, deadlock) at level 3-4 to widen coverage.
4. Use statement-based forms ("which statements about DNS/DHCP/ARP are correct") that the paper did not use.
5. Make Office scenarios multi-condition (mail merge with a data source, pivot with slicer, conditional formatting with a formula rule).
