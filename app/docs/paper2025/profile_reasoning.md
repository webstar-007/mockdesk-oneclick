## 2. Summary

Topic counts (30 questions): 
- Series 5 (Q4, Q7, Q8, Q10, Q29): 4 letter and 1 number.
- Analogy 4 (Q5 letter, Q12 number, Q23 number, Q22 figure).
- Coding-decoding 4 (Q13, Q14, Q19, Q27).
- Maths operations 3 (Q6, Q15, Q16), plus a number table (Q25).
- Blood relations 2 (Q11, Q26).
- Statement logic 3 (Q1 inference, Q18 syllogism, Q17 Venn).
- Word formation 1 (Q3).
- Non-verbal 7 (Q2, Q9, Q20, Q21, Q24, Q28, Q30).

Puzzles and seating: there were none. No seating, ranking, direction, scheduling or calendar sets. No multi-clue puzzle with 8 or more entities. Entity counts were small: the largest blood-relation chain had 5 named people (Q26), the largest coding word had 9 letters. Entities per set: not applicable.

Figure-based vs text-only: 9 figure-based (30%): mirror image 1, paper fold-cut 1, Venn 1, count squares 2, embedded figure 1, figure analogy 1, dice 1, transparent folding 1. That is 8 kinds of figure question, 1 repeated. 2 more (Q25, Q27) are tables that can be written as plain text. So 19 are text-only, 2 are text-izable tables, 9 need images. Text equivalents: Venn can become "choose the set-relationship" among named options. Mirror and counting-squares need ASCII or described grids, or should be replaced by a text analogue.

Coding style: always letter-position coding with consistent shifts. Shifts are constant, alternating (+3/-6), or increasing (+1 to +7). Pair-swap rearrangement. One decode-direction question. One two-matrix lookup. Words are real 6-9 letter words. Options are anagrams or near-misses.

Series style: letter series mostly cyclic with blanks (4 and 8 blanks) or letter-pair groups. Pair series use primes or constant steps in each position. Number series is a single quadratic (8n^2). No mixed-operation series.

Syllogism/statement style: both syllogism and inference use everyday nouns (televisions, radios) and a "treat as true even if contradicting facts" line. Only 2 statements, 2 conclusions. The fall-back answer is "neither", i.e. the converse trap. Only one format of four standard options.

Notably harder than textbook: Q7 (8 blanks), Q10 (prime gaps in two directions), Q12 (n^3 + n), Q22 (figure analogy with transformation plus addition), Q9 (fold-cut), Q25 (sum doubled). Everything else is standard.

Overall level: moderate. Average difficulty about 2.7. Most questions are 1-3 steps, a few are 4-5.

## 3. Rules and ways to be harder

### 10 rules for matching difficulty in text form
1. Use 30 questions with no puzzle/seating set. Spend the slots on one-liners (1-3 steps each).
2. Keep about 30% as "figure-like" items but convert to text: grid counting described by dimensions, Venn by class names, dice by listed faces, mirror image by letter or digit strings.
3. Letter series: one 4-letter cycle or 8-blank pattern, plus one pair-group series with primes or constant steps, plus one group series with mixed directions.
4. Number series stays quadratic or cubic (8n^2, n^3+n), never needs more than 2 steps.
5. Coding: constant, alternating or incrementing shifts with wrap-around. Include one decode-direction question.
6. Include one two-table lookup (row then column, repeated letters).
7. Maths operations: swap-symbol evaluate, swap-two-numbers fix, and choose the correct equation. Keep to 4-6 numbers.
8. Blood relations: 5 or fewer people and at least one irrelevant clue. Gender is inferred.
9. Syllogism and inference: 2 statements, 2 conclusions, the "neither" answer, everyday nouns, standard 4 options.
10. Make all four options look plausible: distractors share the first letters, the right digits in the wrong order, or the wrong direction of an otherwise correct rule.

### 5 ways to be slightly harder
1. Make one analogy use a two-step rule (n^3 + n, then minus the digit sum).
2. Make letter-pair series use primes in one position and Fibonacci in the other.
3. Add a 6-entity seating or ranking set (not in this paper), only if the platform allows it, with 5 clues and one inference that needs combining 2 clues.
4. Use a 3-statement syllogism with a "possibility" conclusion (either-or).
5. Increase blanks in the cyclic letter series to 10, or use a coding with a different shift per position plus a final reversal.
